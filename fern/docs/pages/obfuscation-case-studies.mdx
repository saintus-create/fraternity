# Major Cases of Obfuscation

## Case Studies

<Badge intent="error">7 documented cases</Badge> <Badge intent="info">2001–2024</Badge>

<Callout intent="error" title="Most extensively documented cover-up">
  The **Timothy Piazza** case (Beta Theta Pi, Penn State, 2017) combines nearly every obfuscation pattern: members stepped over an unconscious Piazza, deleted text messages and group chat communications, and waited **12 hours** before calling 911. 18 members were initially charged.

<Accordion title="Tucker Hipps (2014) — Sigma Phi Epsilon, Clemson University">

<Badge intent="warning">No criminal charges</Badge> <Badge intent="info">Wrongful death lawsuit</Badge>

**Organization:** Sigma Phi Epsilon, Clemson University

Tucker Hipps was found dead beneath a bridge during an early morning run with fraternity brothers. Initial reports suggested an accidental fall, but the family's investigation revealed:

- Fraternity members gave **inconsistent accounts** of the morning's events
- Evidence suggested **hazing activity** preceded the death
- The university and fraternity initially characterized it as an accident
- **No criminal charges** were filed despite suspicious circumstances
- A **wrongful death lawsuit** revealed information not in initial reports

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Chad Meredith (2001) — Kappa Sigma, University of Miami">

<Badge intent="note">Initially reported as accident</Badge>

**Organization:** Kappa Sigma, University of Miami

Initially reported as a drowning accident, later investigation revealed fraternity involvement in the events leading to Meredith's death, including alcohol consumption at a fraternity event.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Riley Strain (2024) — Delta Chi, University of Missouri">

<Badge intent="warning">Questions unresolved</Badge>

**Organization:** Delta Chi, University of Missouri

A student who disappeared after being escorted out of a Nashville bar during a fraternity formal event. His body was later found in the Cumberland River. The case raised questions about:

- Fraternity responsibility for member safety during organized events
- How quickly organizations distanced themselves from the incident
- The role of alcohol at sanctioned fraternity events
- Institutional accountability for off-campus fraternity activities

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Michael Deng (2013) — Pi Delta Psi, Baruch College">

<Badge intent="error">Convicted — third-degree murder</Badge>

**Organization:** Pi Delta Psi, Baruch College

Died during a hazing ritual known as the "glass ceiling." The case revealed:

- **Extensive cover-up attempts** by fraternity members
- Delayed medical care while members discussed how to handle the situation
- Evidence of **pre-planned hazing rituals** despite organizational policies prohibiting them
- Multiple convictions including **third-degree murder**

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Daniel Santulli (2021) — Phi Gamma Delta (FIJI), University of Missouri">

<Badge intent="info">Family lawsuit</Badge> <Badge intent="warning">Non-fatal — severe brain damage</Badge>

**Organization:** Phi Gamma Delta (FIJI), University of Missouri

Suffered severe brain damage from a fall after extreme alcohol consumption during a hazing event. The case demonstrated:

- **Surveillance footage** showed fraternity members pouring alcohol on an unconscious Santulli
- Initial organizational response attempted to **minimize hazing characterization**
- The family's lawsuit revealed a pattern of prior incidents at the chapter
- University response criticized as **inadequate and delayed**

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Stone Foltz (2021) — Pi Kappa Alpha, Bowling Green State University">

<Badge intent="error">Criminal charges — involuntary manslaughter</Badge>

**Organization:** Pi Kappa Alpha, Bowling Green State University

Died from alcohol poisoning during a "Big/Little" hazing event. Key obfuscation elements:

- Fraternity members **waited hours** before seeking medical help
- Initial statements downplayed the organized nature of the drinking
- Evidence showed **planned hazing activity** contradicting "voluntary" narratives
- Multiple members faced criminal charges including **involuntary manslaughter**

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Timothy Piazza (2017) — Beta Theta Pi, Penn State University">

<Badge intent="error">Multiple convictions</Badge> <Badge intent="success">Led to anti-hazing law</Badge>

**Organization:** Beta Theta Pi, Penn State University

One of the most extensively documented cases of fraternity cover-up:

- **18 members** initially charged
- Surveillance footage showed members **stepping over** an unconscious Piazza
- Evidence of **deleted text messages** and group chat communications
- Fraternity members waited **12 hours** before calling 911
- The case led to **Pennsylvania's anti-hazing law** (Timothy J. Piazza Anti-Hazing Law)
- Multiple convictions with sentences ranging from probation to jail time
